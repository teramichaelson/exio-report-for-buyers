Owner Background

M. Reyes has held the majority position since founding the business in 2011, joining full time after nine years in contract manufacturing operations. Public filings show no change in registered agent or officer since 2019, which supports a single controlling owner rather than a sponsor-backed structure.

Searches on the principal's name returned two unrelated individuals with similar names, including one with a civil judgment in another state. Neither is connected to this business. We flag it here so it does not surface later in your own diligence as a false positive.

Business Overview

Roughly $14M in trailing revenue across approximately 240 SKUs, manufactured in a leased 41,000 sq ft GMP-certified facility with a second contract manufacturer used for overflow. Headcount is 62, weighted toward production and fulfillment.

Channel mix is the story. Marketplace sales appear to carry the majority of volume, with a smaller owned direct channel and two regional retail accounts added in the last three years. Review counts and rating trajectory across platforms are consistent with steady rather than accelerating demand.

Owner Intelligence: Call Prep

Two data points shape how this conversation should open. First, the owner has held a single controlling position for fourteen years with no outside capital, which usually means valuation expectations are anchored to what the business has paid out rather than to sector multiples. Second, a trade publication interview from last year included remarks about wanting to stay involved in product development. Read that as a preference for structure over headline price.

The practical implication: lead with deal structure, not with a number. An earnout or a rollover position is more likely to move this seller than a higher all-cash offer, and framing it that way early will separate you from buyers who open with a multiple.

Red Flags & Deal Risks

Channel concentration. If marketplace sales carry the majority of revenue, the business is exposed to fee changes, algorithm shifts, and account suspension risk it does not control. Expect this to compress the multiple by one to two turns unless the owned channel is larger than it appears from outside.

Facility lease. The manufacturing lease is the operational asset, and the term is not public. A lease expiring inside the deal window, or one with a change-of-control clause, becomes your problem at closing. Verify before you sign an LOI, not after.

Product liability tail. Supplement manufacturers carry claims exposure that outlives the sale. Any structure here needs a survival period and an escrow sized to the category, not to the deal.

Key person dependency. With 62 employees and a founder still in product development, the question is whether formulation knowledge is documented or resident in one person. This is the single most common reason deals in this sector reprice after diligence.

Sector Tailwinds

Category demand has held up through the last two consumer cycles, with buyers increasingly treating supplements as a recurring rather than episodic purchase. That behavior supports valuation for businesses with demonstrable repeat rates.

Consolidation is active. Both strategic acquirers and platform sponsors have been assembling multi-brand portfolios, and owned manufacturing capacity is treated as a premium asset rather than a liability in that thesis.

Sector Headwinds

Labeling and claims scrutiny remains the defining regulatory risk in the category, and class action activity has stayed elevated. Diligence should assume this exposure exists until documentation proves otherwise.

Customer acquisition costs across direct channels have risen materially. Businesses that grew on cheap acquisition and have not built retention are repricing, and lenders have noticed.

Comparable Transactions

Four transactions in the last thirty months involved supplement businesses with owned manufacturing and a marketplace-weighted channel mix. Disclosed enterprise values ranged from roughly $8M to $46M. Where multiples were reported or reconstructible, they clustered between 4.5x and 7.0x EBITDA, with the upper end reserved for businesses showing documented subscription revenue above 25 percent of sales.

No transaction in that set closed above 7.0x without either a subscription base or a national retail account under contract. That is the bar this target would need to clear to justify the top of the range.

Estimated EBITDA

Supplement manufacturers at this scale with in-house production typically run 14 to 20 percent EBITDA margins. Applied to roughly $14M in revenue, that implies a range of approximately $2.0M to $2.8M.

The variables that move it: how much production runs through the contract manufacturer rather than the owned facility, marketplace fee load as a share of gross, and whether the owner is taking a market-rate salary or a distribution.

Valuation Range

At an estimated $2.4M EBITDA midpoint and a 4.5x to 7.0x range, enterprise value lands between roughly $10.8M and $16.8M.

Channel concentration and an unverified lease both push toward the lower half until resolved. A documented subscription base or a retail contract with term remaining would be the two things that credibly move this into the upper half.

Litigation & Regulatory

We searched federal and state court records, regulatory enforcement databases, and the accreditation complaint file. Results: one closed contract dispute with a former distributor, settled in 2022 with no disclosed terms, and no active litigation naming the entity. No warning letters, no recalls, no regulatory disciplinary actions on record.

The complaint file shows a documented pattern around shipping delays and return processing. Most entries resolved, but the volume relative to company size suggests support infrastructure has not scaled with revenue. That is an operational finding rather than a legal one, and it will show up in diligence as a working capital question.

Clarify on the call. Ask directly whether any claim has been noticed but not filed. The absence of a docket is not the absence of exposure, and sellers in this category often know about something six months before it becomes public.

Sector Vocabulary
GMP-certified
Facility meeting federal Good Manufacturing Practice standards for supplement production. Table stakes for any acquirer with a retail channel.
COA
Certificate of Analysis. Third-party lab verification of potency and purity for a given production lot. Ask how many lots have one.
SKU depth
Count of distinct product variants. High depth can signal category coverage or it can signal unpruned inventory. Check velocity per SKU.
Subscribe rate
Share of orders on recurring delivery. The single strongest driver of multiple in this category.
Fee load
Marketplace commissions, fulfillment, and storage as a share of gross sales. Typically compresses contribution margin by ten to fifteen points.
Questions For The First Call
1

What percentage of revenue comes from the marketplace channel versus your owned site and retail accounts, and how has that mix moved over the last three years?

2

What share of orders are on recurring delivery, and what is the twelve-month retention rate on those subscribers?

3

When does the facility lease expire, and does it contain a change-of-control provision?

4

How much production runs through your own facility versus the contract manufacturer, and what would it cost to bring that volume in-house?

5

If you stepped away in ninety days, who owns formulation, supplier relationships, and marketplace account management? Is any of that documented?

6

Are the last three years reviewed or audited, and have you been through a quality of earnings process before?

7

What would you want your role to look like eighteen months after closing?
